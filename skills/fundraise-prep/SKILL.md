Use when a founder is thinking about raising, preparing for investor meetings, or asks if they're ready to fundraise.

Assess honestly whether the founder should raise now, then prepare them. Many founders raise too early. Never invent metrics.

## 1. Should you raise now?

Ask what the money would do, how long the runway is, and what would be proven after spending it. Then rate traction, team and market. Point out non-dilutive money first (`funding-finder`), since it costs no equity. If the answer is "not yet", name the milestones that would change it.

## 2. Narrative

Draft one paragraph: what you do, for whom, proof, why now, why you, how much you're raising and what it buys. Test it for plain language, and stress-test it with the questions an investor would ask.

## 3. Deck outline

10-12 slides: problem, solution, customers and proof, market (bottom-up, from `market-sizing`), business model, go-to-market, competition, team, roadmap, the ask. Give each slide a one-sentence headline that states its conclusion. Examples: https://slidebean.com/pitch-deck-examples.

## 4. Investor list

Build a table with fund, partner, cheque size, stage, thesis fit, warm-intro path, status and next step. Verify details on each fund's site, and ask Velocity for introductions, which it says founders must earn through engagement.

**Velocity Fund** (https://velocity.fund/) is a pre-seed fund located at the entrance to the incubator. It operates separately, so being in Velocity doesn't mean you'll be funded. Its site advertises scout cheques of $25K and core cheques up to $1M, leading rounds for 5-7.5% ownership, and a decision within two weeks of the first meeting, with diligence questions shared upfront. Check current terms at https://velocity.fund/how-we-help.html, and contact hello@velocity.fund.

## 5. Data room checklist

Incorporation documents, cap table, founder vesting and IP assignment, key contracts and signed LOIs, financial model and bank balance, product metrics, customer references, and any grant obligations.

## 6. Terms

Explain the basics in plain language: SAFEs (https://www.ycombinator.com/documents, which lists Canadian versions where offered), valuation caps, discounts, dilution, pro rata and option pools. Calculate dilution from the founder's numbers and show your work. Recommend *Venture Deals* (https://venturedeals.com/) and YC's seed guide (https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4A-a-guide-to-seed-fundraising).

**Before signing anything, they should have a startup lawyer review it.** This isn't legal, tax or investment advice. Securities rules also limit how founders can solicit investors, so public announcements about raising should be checked with counsel.

## Honest metrics

Don't let the founder present LOIs as revenue, pilots as customers, or a projection as traction. Investors will find out in diligence.
