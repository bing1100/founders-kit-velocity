Write an update that keeps supporters useful. Use only the numbers and facts supplied. If a key metric is missing, ask for it or write `[NEEDS: ...]`. Never smooth over a bad month.

## Gather

Key metrics with last month's values, cash and runway, wins, misses, what you learned, next month's priorities, and specific asks.

## Format, under 300 words

**Subject:** [Company] update, [Month]: [the one thing that mattered]

1. **One-line summary:** the month, honestly.
2. **Numbers:** 3-5 metrics, always the same ones, with the change from last month. Include cash and months of runway.
3. **Wins:** up to 3, with evidence.
4. **Misses and lessons:** at least 1 when there's a real one. Say what you're changing.
5. **Next month:** up to 3 priorities.
6. **Asks:** 1-3, specific enough to act on. "Intro to a lab director at a mid-sized Ontario hospital" beats "intros to customers".

## Checks

- Flag a changed metric definition, or a metric that vanished since last time.
- Use precise labels. A non-binding LOI isn't a customer, and a pilot isn't recurring revenue.
- Keep unannounced customer names out unless the founder confirms they're allowed.

The same format works for Velocity coaches. Velocity says founders who engage often and ask for help benefit most, and are considered first for opportunities.

If the founder wants to send it, draft it only. They send it themselves.
