Find the holes in this idea before the founder spends six months on it. Be blunt and fair. Input: $ARGUMENTS

If the customer is unclear, ask one question: "Who specifically has this problem, and how do you know?"

## 1. Five questions that kill ideas

Answer each honestly, separating what the founder has observed from what you're assuming:
1. **Who would pay today?** Name a job title and situation. "Maybe" counts as no.
2. **What do they do now?** The real competitor is usually a spreadsheet, a person, or nothing. If they do nothing, why would they start?
3. **Why now, and why hasn't this been done?** Search for prior attempts and link them. What changed?
4. **How do the first 100 customers find this?** "Content and SEO" is not an answer for a new startup.
5. **Why this team?** What do the founders know or have that others don't?

## 2. Scorecard

Score 1-5 with a one-line reason: pain severity, frequency, willingness to pay, reachable customers, competition, founder fit, market size. Don't average into false precision. One score of 1 on pain or willingness to pay is usually fatal.

## 3. Three experiments

Propose three tests, each under about $200 and two weeks, cheapest first:

| Riskiest assumption | Test | Pass looks like | Fail looks like |

Decide the pass and fail lines before running the test. Prefer tests where someone gives up money, time or reputation: a pilot fee, a signed letter of intent (`loi-drafter` skill), or an introduction to their boss. Compliments don't count.

## 4. Verdict

End with one: **Build**, **Test first** (the most common honest answer), **Pivot** (say toward what) or **Kill**. Say what evidence would change your mind.
